# Pilot Study Questionnaire — Study 1

Completed by participants after experiencing each system. The questionnaire combines standardized scales with researcher-custom items, using a unified 7-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 7 = Strongly Agree) to measure subjective experience.

> **Translation note:** This English version is the primary version. Items marked **(R)** are reverse-scored; compute `8 − raw score` before including in subscale averages.

---

## A. Content Quality

### A1 Narrative Quality

**Narrative Engagement Scale** (Busselle & Bilandzic, 2009; full 12-item scale)

> Rate each statement on a 1–7 scale. Items are presented in random order; words like "story / plot / character" may be replaced to match the specific stimulus material.

**Narrative Understanding**
1. I had a hard time recognizing the thread of the story. **(R)**
2. At points, I had a hard time making sense of what was going on in the story. **(R)**
3. My understanding of the characters is unclear. **(R)**

**Attentional Focus**

4. I found my mind wandering while experiencing the story. **(R)**
5. While the story was on, I found myself thinking about other things. **(R)**
6. I had a hard time keeping my mind on the story. **(R)**

**Narrative Presence**

7. At times during the story, the story world was closer to me than the real world.
8. During the story, my body was in the room, but my mind was inside the world created by the story.
9. The story created a new world, and then that world suddenly disappeared when the story ended.

**Emotional Engagement**

10. The story affected me emotionally.
11. During the story, when a main character succeeded, I felt happy; when they suffered in some way, I felt sad.
12. I felt sorry for some of the characters in the story.

**Researcher-custom item (example)**
- The story presents diverse viewpoints and conflicting interests across multiple characters, enhancing narrative tension and persuasiveness.

---

### A2 Explanation Quality

**Explanation Satisfaction Scale** (Hoffman, Mueller, Klein, & Litman, 2023; full scale)

> The original scale uses a 5-point Likert; this study standardizes to 7 points. "[system/tool/algorithm]" in brackets may be replaced with the specific research object.

1. From the explanation, I know how the [system/tool] works.
2. This explanation of how the [system/tool] works is satisfying.
3. This explanation of how the [system/tool] works has sufficient detail.
4. This explanation of how the [system/tool] works seems complete.
5. This explanation of how the [system/tool] works tells me how to use it.
6. This explanation of how the [system/tool] works is useful to my goals.
7. This explanation of the [system/tool] shows me how accurate the [system/tool] is.

**Researcher-custom item (example)**
- The alternative historical paths resulting from my choices feel plausible within the era's context.

## B. Interaction

### B1 Sense of Agency

**PENS — Autonomy / Perceived Choice Subscale** (Ryan, Rigby, & Przybylski, 2006)

> The PENS autonomy subscale commonly uses 3 core items (Immersyve commercial version). The sample items from Ryan et al. (2006) are also included below for optional use.

**Core Items**
1. The [game/system] provides me with interesting options and choices.
2. The [game/system] lets you do interesting things.
3. I experienced a lot of freedom in the [game/system].

**Ryan et al. (2006) Sample Items (5-item version, optional)**

4. I did things in [the experience] because they interested me.
5. I felt controlled and pressured to be a certain way. **(R)**

---

### B2 Usability

**UMUX-Lite** (Lewis, Utesch, & Maher, 2013; full scale)

1. [This system]'s capabilities meet my requirements.
2. [This system] is easy to use.

**Paas Mental Effort Single-Item Scale** (Paas, 1994; single item)

> The original scale uses 9 points (1 = very, very low mental effort ~ 9 = very, very high mental effort). If standardizing to a 7-point Likert, use the reworded version below; to preserve the original format, the 9-point scale is recommended.

**Single item (recommended: retain 9-point original)**
- How much mental effort did you invest in completing this task?
  - 1 = Very, very low
  - 9 = Very, very high

**Or: 7-point Likert reworded version**
- Completing this task required a great deal of mental effort.

**Researcher-custom item (example)**
- Experiencing the story from other characters' viewpoints provides me with new information and understanding.

## D. Motivation

**Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI; Ryan, 1982)** — Subscales: Interest/Enjoyment (Motivation), Value/Usefulness (Value), Pressure/Tension (Negative Affect)

> Ryan (1982) treats "Interest/Enjoyment" as the core self-report indicator of intrinsic motivation, so the motivation subscale corresponds to IMI **Interest/Enjoyment**; value corresponds to **Value/Usefulness**; negative affect corresponds to **Pressure/Tension**.

### Motivation — Interest/Enjoyment

1. I enjoyed doing this activity very much.
2. This activity was fun to do.
3. I thought this was a boring activity. **(R)**
4. This activity did not hold my attention at all. **(R)**
5. I would describe this activity as very interesting.
6. I thought this activity was quite enjoyable.
7. While I was doing this activity, I was thinking about how much I enjoyed it.

### Value/Usefulness

8. I believe this activity could be of some value to me.
9. I think that doing this activity is useful for ______.
10. I think this is important to do because it can ______.
11. I would be willing to do this again because it has some value to me.
12. I think doing this activity could help me to ______.
13. I believe doing this activity could be beneficial to me.
14. I think this is an important activity.

> The blanks in items 9, 10, and 12 should be completed based on the specific research objective (e.g., "deepen understanding of historical narratives," "cultivate critical reading skills"). If no specific outcome is targeted, items may be shortened to "…is useful / important / helpful."

### Negative Affect — Pressure/Tension

15. I did not feel nervous at all while doing this. **(R)**
16. I felt very tense while doing this activity.
17. I was very relaxed in doing these. **(R)**
18. I was anxious while working on this task.
19. I felt pressured while doing these.

## Scoring Notes

- **Reverse scoring:** For items marked **(R)**, compute `8 − raw score` before including in subscale averages.
- **NES (Narrative Engagement Scale):** In the original 12-item paper, all items in the "Narrative Understanding" and "Attentional Focus" subscales are reverse-scored (presented as negatively worded statements).
- **PENS Autonomy:** Take the average of all items in the subscale; if using the 5-item Ryan (2006) version, reverse-score the reverse item (e.g., item 5).
- **UMUX-Lite:** Original formula = `((Item1 − 1) + (Item2 − 1)) / 12 × 100`, yielding a 0–100 score; if using only as a 7-point Likert average, the mean of the two items may be used directly.
- **Paas Mental Effort:** Single item, use raw score directly.
- **IMI:** Compute the mean of each subscale separately; higher Pressure/Tension scores indicate greater stress.
