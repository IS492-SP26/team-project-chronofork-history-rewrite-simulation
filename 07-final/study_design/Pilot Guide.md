# Pilot Study: Narrative & Mechanism Validation (Web vs. IDN)

## Goal

Study 1 aims to independently validate the effectiveness of ChronoEngine's instant historical narrative generation and rewriting components. We will conduct a within-subject comparison with a system built using Twine, a traditional interactive digital narrative (IDN) standard tool, examining how mechanisms such as multi-branch exploration, instant rewriting/narrative generation, and checkpoint backtracking affect user experience compared to predefined static narratives.

## Method

We plan to recruit 6 college students (approximately 18–25 years old, any gender and major) to participate in the study. Participants will sign up voluntarily through campus posters and online communities. All participants will sign an informed consent form before the experiment, clearly understanding the research procedure and agreeing to have their data (interaction logs, questionnaires, and interview records) used for research analysis. Participants will receive $10/h compensation upon completing the experiment.

This study uses a within-subject design. Each participant will experience two different system conditions: ① ChronoFork-Web dynamic interaction system (powered by ChronoEngine, with a minimalist web interface to expose core functionality; users can freely choose actions or directly input proposals at key nodes to change the course of history), and ② a static predefined narrative baseline (an interactive story built on the Twine platform, with content drafted offline using the same story engine and reviewed and refined by experts; users' choices are limited to preset options). This condition is designed to contrast ChronoEngine's experience with the typical linear/preset interactive narrative convention. To avoid learning effects and preference bias, each participant can select two historical events of interest from our prepared Event Library ([Event Library]), randomly assigned to the two system conditions respectively, with system order counterbalanced. To control variables and ensure rigor, the canonical historical timeline for both systems is pre-generated by ChronoEngine and verified against historical facts by experts before the experiment, with potential errors recorded and corrected if necessary. This verification record will also be used to evaluate ChronoEngine's metric (A1.1 Historical Fidelity).

## Procedure

The experiment is conducted individually in a quiet laboratory. The entire session lasts approximately 60 minutes: the researcher first introduces the experimental procedure to participants and obtains consent. Participants then select 2 historical events of interest from the Event Library, which the researcher randomly assigns to the two system interfaces. Before the experience, participants learn the basic operation of the corresponding system through a video tutorial. Once they understand, participants begin the interactive experience of the first historical event (system order as assigned). Under both conditions, participants will first experience the canonical historical path, after which they may freely explore different branches. Under the ChronoFork-Web condition, participants can return to any node or switch character perspectives, freely interact with other characters, and explore different instantly generated branches. Under the Twine condition, participants can also try different branches by selecting different options. Each scenario experience lasts approximately 15 minutes, but participants may end early if they feel they have explored sufficiently. During the experiment, participants are encouraged to think aloud, and the system automatically records user operation logs. The researcher observes without interfering and provides necessary assistance.

After completing the first scenario, participants fill out a questionnaire evaluating their experience with the system across various dimensions ([Questionnaire-Study1]). The questionnaire includes standardized scale items and custom question items. Completing the questionnaire takes approximately 5 minutes. The researcher can clarify any doubts about concepts covered in the questionnaire. After a 2–3 minute break, participants begin the tutorial and interactive experience for the second historical event, using the other system condition (same process as above, but with a different system and story). Similarly, approximately 15 minutes of exploration time is allowed. Participants then complete a second post-condition questionnaire (same content as the first).

After experiencing both systems and completing the questionnaires, the researcher conducts a semi-structured interview with participants (approximately 20 minutes, [Interview-Study1]). The interview focuses on comparing the narrative quality and interaction features of the two systems, gaining deeper insight into subjective feelings and specific examples that cannot be fully captured by questionnaires. The interview will be recorded and archived, then transcribed for qualitative analysis. After the experiment, the researcher organizes the questionnaire, log data, and interview recordings; any personal identifying information will be deleted or anonymized during transcription and organization.

## Interview Guide

After completing both conditions' experience and questionnaires, we will conduct an in-depth **semi-structured interview** to qualitatively gather detailed user feedback on narrative quality and interaction mechanisms. Interview questions are designed around dimensions A, B, and E, encouraging participants to elaborate on their views in their own words. The following is an outline of the key interview questions:

1. **Overall Impression and Preference (Warm-up)**
   - Looking back at the two systems you used today, how would you rate the overall experience of each?
     - In your view, what are the strengths and weaknesses of each system?
   - Were there any moments that left a strong impression on you? Can you give an example?
     - Why?

2. **Narrative Content and Quality (A1)**
   - In the narratives of both systems, were you able to understand why the story developed the way it did? [Causal Logic]
     - What did you use to judge causal coherence? Can you give an example?
     - Which system made it easier for you to understand the reasons behind the story's development?
       - Why?
   - Please choose one character from each of the 2 experiences that left the strongest impression, and explain why they appealed to you. [Character Performance]
     - How did this appeal affect your experience?
     - Did you find the behavior of historical figures in the story to be reasonable and consistent?
       - Were there any moments where you felt pulled out of immersion? What was inconsistent?
   - Did you sense competition or conflict among multiple parties during either experience, or that different characters had different viewpoints? [Multiple Perspectives]
     - How did the experience of multiple perspectives affect your overall experience?
     - Was there a difference between the two systems in terms of multiple perspectives?
       - Where was it reflected? What caused it?

3. **Branch and Narrative Plausibility (A2)**
   - Were there any memorable hypothetical historical branches? Tell us why they were memorable. [Branch Credibility]
     - Did you find them plausible? How did you judge?
     - Which system had better quality in this regard? Why?
   - In the interactive system, when you attempted certain changes, the system provided some explanations. [Feasibility Analysis Credibility]
     - Did you find these explanations sufficient and credible? Why?
     - Were they helpful in understanding decision-making in historical events? In what way?

4. **Sense of User Control (B1)**
   - Which system gave you more of a "I'm shaping the story" feeling? [Perceived Control]
     - Where was it reflected? Why?
     - How did this sense of shaping the story affect your experience?
   - Did you feel that your choices genuinely influenced different outcomes? [Impact Meaningfulness]
     - How did you sense this?
     - Was there a difference between the two systems in this regard?
     - How did this sense of decision impact affect your experience?
   - Which system's branch exploration feature did you prefer? [Exploration Fluidity]
     - Why?

5. **Mechanism Effectiveness (B2)**
   - Did you go back to any previous decision points during your experience? How did you feel about that feature? [Backtracking]
     - Why did you feel that way?
     - Which of the two systems' different backtracking designs do you prefer?
       - Why? How does backtracking affect the experience?
     - Were you able to understand the story graph in the middle?
       - How did it help you make decisions?
   - Did you switch to other characters' perspectives during your experience? How did you feel about that feature? [Perspective Switch]
     - Why did you feel that way?
     - How does switching perspectives affect the experience?
   - Did you use the hint feature during your experience? [Guidance]
     - Where were you stuck? What did you hope to get from it?
     - How does the hint feature affect the experience?

6. **System Usability (B3)**
   - Were you able to clearly distinguish whether the current narrative was the canonical history in both systems? [Divergence Clarity]
     - Which did you prefer? Why?
   - How did you find the interaction pace and response speed of the systems?
     - In your view, what should a "comfortably paced" interaction feel like?

7. **Emotional Experience (E)**
   - Overall, which system did you find more fun and enjoyable? [Interest/Enjoyment]
     - What was the reason (what made it fun)?
     - How did this sense of fun affect your experience?
   - Was there a difference in your curiosity and desire to explore between the two systems? [Curiosity]
     - Why?
   - Which narrative approach do you prefer? [Narrative]
     - Why?
     - How do different narrative approaches affect the experience?
   - Was there a difference between the two systems in terms of character expressiveness? [Character]
     - Where was it reflected?
     - How did character expressiveness affect your experience?
   - Were there moments during the experience when you felt frustrated or overwhelmed? [Frustration]
     - Please elaborate on where you got stuck and how you resolved it.
   - Through experiencing this historical event in both ways, do you feel your historical understanding has improved? [Educational Value]
     - For example, did you learn new historical facts or new perspectives? What did you learn?
   - Do you think the dynamic interaction of the interactive system offers unique benefits for learning history or developing thinking skills?
     - By comparison, what did the static version offer you?
