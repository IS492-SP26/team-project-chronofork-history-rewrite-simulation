# Inductive Thematic Analysis: ChronoFork vs. IDN-Twine Pilot Study

**Method:** Braun & Clarke's 6-phase reflexive thematic analysis (inductive approach)
**Data:** Semi-structured interviews with 6 participants (P1–P6)
**Analyst note:** Themes emerged inductively from the data rather than being mapped onto pre-existing questionnaire dimensions. All six transcripts were read multiple times, initial codes were generated, and codes were iteratively grouped into candidate themes, reviewed, refined, and named. P2/P5 originally shared one transcript (戊戌变法 vs. 古巴导弹危机 ChronoFork sessions); P3/P6 originally shared one transcript (赤壁之战 vs. 泰坦尼克号 ChronoFork sessions). They are reported separately here to align with the 6 participant questionnaires.

## 2. Theme Definitions

### Theme 1: From Reader to Author — The Agency Divide

**Definition:** Participants consistently distinguished between two qualitatively different modes of engagement: one in which they felt they were *making* the story (ChronoFork) and one in which they were *selecting among pre-made outcomes* (IDN-Twine). This theme captures the felt difference in creative authorship and its downstream effects on motivation, immersion, and sustained interest.

**Scope:** Encompasses perceived control, the meaningfulness of choices, and the relationship between interaction openness and exploration motivation. It does not cover specific narrative quality judgments (those fall under Theme 3).

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6

---

### Theme 2: Embodied Understanding — Role-play as Epistemic Tool

**Definition:** When participants inhabited a historical figure's perspective (possible only in ChronoFork), they reported a qualitatively different kind of historical understanding: not knowing *what* happened, but feeling *why* it had to happen that way. This theme captures role-play as a vehicle for historical empathy and counterfactual reasoning, as well as the limits of that vehicle when exploration becomes mechanical.

**Scope:** Covers perspective-switching, the educational value of counterfactual experimentation, and the contrast with IDN-Twine's more informational learning mode. It also includes the negative case where IDN-Twine exploration devolved into "collecting endings."

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6

---

### Theme 3: Showing vs. Telling — Two Registers of Historical Narrative

**Definition:** The two systems employ fundamentally different narrative registers: ChronoFork *dramatizes* history through multi-character dialogue and real-time debate, while IDN-Twine *narrates* it through polished, omniscient prose. Participants recognized trade-offs in each: dramatization produces deeper engagement but heavier cognitive load; narration is efficient and clear but emotionally thinner.

**Scope:** Covers narrative voice, textual density, the cognitive demands of extended reading, and the distinction between process-oriented and conclusion-oriented storytelling.

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6

---

### Theme 4: Character Fidelity and the Uncanny — When AI Breaks the Spell

**Definition:** Participants were attentive to whether AI-generated characters behaved in historically and psychologically plausible ways. When characters acted consistently with their historical personas, immersion deepened. When they deviated — through anachronistic reactions, personality inconsistency, or formulaic AI-generated prose — participants experienced "breaking the spell" moments that undermined trust and engagement.

**Scope:** Covers character plausibility, persona consistency, the specific failure modes of AI character generation (e.g., refusing gifts that a character should accept, being inappropriately defiant toward authority), and the contrast with IDN-Twine's pre-authored but less vivid characters.

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6 (P2, P3, P5 most acute persona-break instances; P6 mild)

---

### Theme 5: Convergence Forces — Freedom, Narrative Gravity, and Design Intent

**Definition:** Participants experienced tension between their desire to explore divergent historical paths and the system's tendency to pull the narrative back toward canonical history. This convergence was interpreted differently depending on framing: as a design flaw (preventing genuine exploration), as a meaningful statement about historical inevitability, or as a consequence of AI limitations. Separately, IDN-Twine's abrupt dead-ends produced a distinct form of frustration-cum-surprise.

**Scope:** Covers the "correction force" phenomenon from a structural rather than character-level perspective, IDN-Twine's sudden bad endings, and participants' desire for clearer outcome comparison between their path and the historical record.

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6

---

### Theme 6: Scaffolding and Onboarding — The Cost of Complexity

**Definition:** ChronoFork's richer interaction model came with usability costs: an unfamiliar interface, longer response times, and a steeper learning curve. Participants balanced these costs against the system's depth, generally concluding that the depth was worth the friction — but that the friction was real and addressable.

**Scope:** Covers UI discoverability, response latency and its effect on attention, the role of guidance/hint features, and specific improvement suggestions.

**Contributing participants:** P1, P2, P4, P5, P6 (P3 did not raise onboarding concerns)

## 3. Cross-System Comparison Matrix

| Theme | ChronoFork (Dynamic AI-Generated) | IDN-Twine (Static Predefined) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Agency Divide** | Participants felt like "authors" shaping the story; open-ended text input created a sense of genuine creative participation (P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6). Choices produced unpredictable consequences, sustaining curiosity across sessions. | Participants felt like "selectors" choosing among fixed options (all 6). After exhausting all branches, exploration motivation collapsed into mechanical "collection" (P1, P6). P2: "Why not just read a history book?" |
| **2. Embodied Understanding** | Role-playing as historical figures (Zhou Yu, Guangxu Emperor, Sun Quan, Tojo Hideki, Cuban President, Captain Smith) produced empathic insight into decision-making constraints (P1, P2, P4, P5). Counterfactual experiments revealed *why* historical actors chose as they did (P1, P4). | Learning was informational rather than experiential — participants gained factual knowledge (e.g., 162 tactical nuclear warheads in Cuba, P4) but did not report perspective-taking or empathic understanding. |
| **3. Narrative Register** | Multi-voice dialogue dramatized debate and made causal reasoning visible through process, not summary (P1, P4). However, text volume was high and reading fatigue was a common complaint (P1, P2). | Polished omniscient narration was efficient and clear (P1, P4). Specific counter-factual write-ups were praised as "genuinely chilling" (P4). But the narrative felt like "a textbook" (P1) or "pre-recorded video" (P4). |
| **4. Character Fidelity** | Characters were "alive" — they argued, hesitated, and challenged the player (P4). But persona breaks occurred: Khrushchev reversing position illogically (P5), Ronglu being disrespectful to the Emperor (P2), Cao Cao refusing gifts he should accept (P3), Titanic crew offering no resistance to safety-first orders (P6). Formulaic sentence patterns reduced realism (P4). | Characters were consistent (pre-authored) but flat — serving as "spokespeople" delivering one line before passing control back to the player (P1, P4). Less immersive but no immersion-breaking moments. |
| **5. Convergence Forces** | Narrative convergence toward canonical history frustrated some (P2, P3, P5) but was interpreted by others as a meaningful "fate" mechanic (P3). Players wanted to diverge more radically than the system allowed (P2: assassination of Cixi; P3: Sun Quan's surrender; P5: Cuban defiance toward US). P6 wanted clearer outcome-comparison panels. | Abrupt dead-ends were surprising — sometimes pleasantly (P1's "bad end" in Humen), sometimes frustratingly (P6's one-second war ending). Outcomes were clear but predetermined. |
| **6. Scaffolding** | Interface required learning: backtracking buttons were hard to find (P1, P4), timeline UI was confusing (P1, P2, P5). Response latency caused attentional drift (P4). Hint features were appreciated by novices (P1) but bypassed by experts (P2, P4, P5). | Interface was immediately intuitive — "clean and straightforward" (P4). Near-instant response. No onboarding needed. But simplicity came at the cost of depth. |

## 4. Representative Quotes

### Theme 1: From Reader to Author — The Agency Divide

> **P1:** "第二个系统玩了两三个结局之后，后面就只是在做一种收集工作，我只是想把每个结局都走一遍而已。我其实都没有太关注故事真正在讲什么了，我只是机械性的把没选过的选项选一遍。"
> *"After playing two or three endings in the second system [IDN-Twine], I was just doing collection work — I only wanted to walk through every ending. I wasn't really paying attention to what the story was actually saying anymore; I was just mechanically selecting every option I hadn't picked."*

> **P2:** "第一种的话，那我为什么不直接去看历史书呢？第二种肯定更好玩一点。"
> *"If it's the first kind [IDN-Twine], why wouldn't I just go read a history book? The second kind [ChronoFork] is definitely more fun."*

> **P4:** "差别就像吃菜单上的两道菜 vs 自己点单。"
> *"The difference is like choosing between two dishes on a set menu vs. ordering whatever you want."*

> **P4:** "让我从'读者'变成了'作者'。我在ChronoFork里会主动思考'如果是我，会怎么劝罗斯福'，而Twine里我只是在猜'哪个选项是正史'。"
> *"It turned me from a 'reader' into an 'author.' In ChronoFork I would actively think 'if it were me, how would I persuade Roosevelt,' whereas in Twine I was just guessing 'which option is the real history.'"*

---

### Theme 2: Embodied Understanding — Role-play as Epistemic Tool

> **P1:** "体验了不同的反事实的剧情之后，我其实更能理解当时正史的情况下，为什么那些历史人物要做出那样的决策了。就是因为做其他的决策，要么可行性不高，要么就是他们缺少一些信息，他们没有办法想到。"
> *"After experiencing different counterfactual storylines, I actually understood better why those historical figures made those decisions in real history. It's because other decisions were either not feasible, or they lacked certain information and couldn't have thought of them."*

> **P4:** "当我作为东条想'撤军会不会让军部哗变'时，我就开始理解为什么他在历史上没退步——这是看Twine时永远体会不到的。"
> *"When, as Tojo, I thought 'would withdrawing troops cause a military mutiny,' I began to understand why he didn't back down in history — that's something you can never experience through Twine."*

> **P2:** "现成的历史都是死的，死知识。但是你们这个系统因为是实时去算的一个剧情，所以我需要相当于临场应变。"
> *"Established history is dead — dead knowledge. But because your system calculates the plot in real-time, I need to improvise on the spot."*

---

### Theme 3: Showing vs. Telling — Two Registers of Historical Narrative

> **P4:** "Twine是把因果浓缩成几句旁白告诉我的……ChronoFork则是让我亲眼看到东条英机和山本五十六、川崎清一来一回地讨论……我是从这种你来我往的对话里自己理出因果链的。"
> *"Twine condensed causality into a few lines of narration... ChronoFork let me watch Tojo Hideki and Yamamoto Isoroku and Kawasaki go back and forth in discussion... I constructed the causal chain myself from that give-and-take dialogue."*

> **P4:** "Twine的叙事让你'懂'，ChronoFork的叙事让你'在场'。前者偏知识传递，后者偏情境带入。"
> *"Twine's narrative makes you 'understand'; ChronoFork's narrative makes you 'be there.' The former leans toward knowledge transfer, the latter toward situational immersion."*

> **P1:** "第一个系统的话字太多了，读起来比较累。第二个系统的话他那个文字量可能少一点，就读起来比较轻松。"
> *"The first system [ChronoFork] had way too much text — it was tiring to read. The second system [IDN-Twine] had less text, so it was more relaxing to read."*

### Theme 4: Character Fidelity and the Uncanny — When AI Breaks the Spell

> **P5:** "我作为古巴总统，想要让苏联在古巴布设核防御……我跟他表态说我不听美国的，这个时候赫鲁晓夫居然没有顺着我的意思去说——他把核弹运过来的呀，现在我跟他说可以再运点过来，他居然还反悔了。这个太脱离人设了。"
> *"As the Cuban president, I wanted the Soviet Union to set up nuclear defenses in Cuba... I told him I won't listen to the Americans, and then Khrushchev didn't go along with me — he had shipped the nukes over in the first place, and now when I told him to ship more, he actually backtracked. That was completely out of character."*

> **P6:** "我一旦说安全，大家全部跟着我走，一切为了安全考虑。就只对这个泰坦尼克来说，应该当我说安全的时候，应该有一个阻力——比如说现在有什么公司老总，他要追求速度。就我应该有一个阻力的。"
> *"As soon as I said 'safety first,' everyone fell in line — everything for safety. But for Titanic specifically, when I said safety, there should have been pushback — like a company executive wanting speed. There should have been resistance."*

> **P3:** "当我送出大小乔的时候，他不应该是明确的拒绝。就是有那种稍微不符合人设的小点——他历史上应该是很开心的，即使他不相信我，这件事情他应该是开心的，反正也不亏。"
> *"When I offered the two Qiao sisters, he [Cao Cao] shouldn't have outright refused. There were small points that didn't fit his persona — historically he should have been pleased; even if he didn't trust me, this was a no-lose situation for him."*

> **P4:** "ChronoFork偶尔会出戏——不是逻辑不对，而是有时候AI生成的对话太'齐整'了，每个角色都用差不多的句式，少了点真人的语病和插嘴。"
> *"ChronoFork occasionally broke immersion — not because the logic was wrong, but because sometimes the AI-generated dialogue was too 'neat'; every character used similar sentence structures, lacking the speech errors and interruptions of real people."*

### Theme 5: Convergence Forces — Freedom, Narrative Gravity, and Design Intent

> **P2:** "只要我的选择偏离历史，他就会尝试把我揪回来，这个感觉不是很好……我就要这么干，我都是光绪帝了，然后下面还一直拦着我不让我这么干，这个很不爽。"
> *"As soon as my choices deviated from history, it would try to pull me back — that didn't feel good... I wanted to do this, I'm the Guangxu Emperor, and the people below kept blocking me from doing it. That was really frustrating."*

> **P6:** "结局是可以知道的——但中间过程，假设我是个不了解的人，我可能确实不知道哪个是真实，哪个是因为我的选择而导致出了新的分支。最后的时候可能会有个比对——比如说正史结局是什么，然后因为我的结局导致什么。可能会有个对比会好一点。"
> *"You can tell what the ending is — but the middle process, if I were someone unfamiliar with the history, I really wouldn't know which is real and which is a new branch caused by my choice. At the end there should be a comparison — what was the canonical ending, what happened because of my choice. A comparison would be better."*

> **P3:** "如果做游戏，收束感是一种设计、一种游戏设计——来告诉我历史有一种向心力。这种'怎么挣扎也找不到正确时间线'的命运感，反而让我更喜欢这个游戏。"
> *"If it's a game, the convergence is a design — a game design — telling me that history has a kind of gravitational pull. This feeling of 'no matter how you struggle, you can't find the right timeline' — that sense of fate actually makes me like the game more."*

> **P1:** "第二个系统，就是虎门销烟的剧情。我选了一个不支持林则徐虎门销烟，然后就直接到达这个结局了。这个让我觉得非常非常的意外……就像突然就这个 bad end 了一样。"
> *"In the second system [IDN-Twine], the Humen Opium Destruction storyline — I chose not to support Lin Zexu, and it went straight to the ending. That was extremely unexpected... it was like suddenly hitting a bad ending."*

### Theme 6: Scaffolding and Onboarding — The Cost of Complexity

> **P4:** "一开始我有点搞不懂界面，不知道怎么回到某个节点，是摸索了一会才找到那个'回溯到该节点'的按钮。"
> *"At first I couldn't quite figure out the interface — I didn't know how to get back to a certain node. I fumbled around for a while before finding the 'backtrack to this node' button."*

> **P2:** "点回溯的那个 UI 不太好，不太方便。就是我要先把他给我推荐的四个点掉，然后我再去左上角选一个节点，然后再去选一个人物。一开始不太习惯。"
> *"The backtracking UI wasn't great, not very convenient. I had to first dismiss the four recommendations, then go to the top-left to select a node, then select a character. It took getting used to."*

> **P1:** "第一个系统它虽然会有的时候要等一会儿，但是总体来说完全可以接受……能够给一些便捷的提示我觉得挺好的，就是选两下就会有一个立竿见影的效果。"
> *"The first system [ChronoFork] sometimes required waiting, but overall it was completely acceptable... Having convenient hints was nice — just two clicks and you'd see an immediate effect."*

## 5. Summary of Key Findings

This pilot study reveals six inductively derived themes that illuminate the experiential and epistemic differences between a dynamic AI-generated interactive historical narrative (ChronoFork) and a static, pre-authored branching narrative (IDN-Twine).

**The most pervasive finding is the agency divide** (Theme 1). All six participants distinguished between *making* history and *selecting* it. ChronoFork's open-ended input transformed participants from passive selectors into active authors, sustaining curiosity far longer. IDN-Twine's finite branch structure, by contrast, led to rapid exhaustion of novelty, with P1 explicitly describing a shift from engaged exploration to mechanical completion. P2's blunt comparison — "why wouldn't I just read a history book?" — captures the perceived redundancy of a system that offers interactivity without genuine openness.

**Role-play emerged as a distinctive epistemic tool** (Theme 2), not merely an engagement feature. When participants inhabited historical figures, they did not simply learn facts; they experienced the *constraints* under which those figures operated. P1's insight — that counterfactual exploration made canonical history more intelligible — and P4's report of understanding Tojo's intransigence only after personally confronting the risk of military mutiny suggest that first-person historical role-play facilitates a form of "constraint-aware" understanding unavailable through passive reading.

**The two systems employ fundamentally different narrative registers** (Theme 3). ChronoFork dramatizes history as multi-voice debate, making causal reasoning visible through process; IDN-Twine narrates it as polished summary, making conclusions efficiently accessible. P4 crystallized this as the difference between "understanding" and "being there." However, ChronoFork's dramatization comes at a cost: substantial text volume that produced reading fatigue across participants.

**Character fidelity is a critical vulnerability of the AI-generated system** (Theme 4). Participants were surprisingly attuned to whether characters' behavior aligned with their known historical dispositions. Specific persona breaks — Khrushchev illogically reversing course (P5), Cao Cao refusing an advantageous gift (P3), Ronglu speaking inappropriately to the Emperor (P2), and Titanic crew showing zero resistance to safety-first orders (P6) — produced immersion-breaking moments. P4 identified a subtler issue: the homogeneity of AI-generated prose, in which all characters spoke with suspiciously similar syntax, lacked the texture of genuine human speech.

**Narrative convergence toward canonical history was the most ambivalently received feature** (Theme 5). P2 and P5 experienced it as outright frustration, interpreting AI characters' resistance to divergence as an illegitimate constraint on agency. P3, however, reframed convergence as a potentially powerful design statement about historical inevitability — a "fate mechanic" that could enhance the experience if implemented intentionally rather than as an artifact of AI limitations. P6 added a complementary critique: regardless of whether convergence is framed as fate or limitation, the system needs an explicit *outcome-comparison panel* at the end so the player can see what their actions changed relative to canonical history. This divergence in interpretation suggests that convergence may need to be explicitly framed to users as either a design feature or a technical limitation, rather than left ambiguous.

**The usability cost of complexity is real but manageable** (Theme 6). ChronoFork's richer interaction model required onboarding that participants did not receive, resulting in fumbling with backtracking mechanisms and timeline navigation. Response latency, while generally tolerable, caused attentional drift during longer waits. However, no participant reported that these usability issues outweighed ChronoFork's experiential advantages — they were annoyances, not dealbreakers.

**A cross-cutting observation** deserves emphasis: the two systems may be more complementary than competitive. P4 explicitly proposed using IDN-Twine as a "framework primer" before engaging with ChronoFork for deep exploration, and P4's remark that "Twine makes you understand; ChronoFork makes you be there" suggests the systems serve different epistemic functions that could be sequenced rather than opposed. This complementarity — static narration for orientation, dynamic role-play for depth — is a finding with direct design implications for future iterations.
